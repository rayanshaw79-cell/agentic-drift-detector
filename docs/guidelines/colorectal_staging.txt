COLORECTAL CANCER STAGING — NCI/AJCC 8TH EDITION SUMMARY

SOURCE: National Cancer Institute PDQ; AJCC Cancer Staging Manual 8th Edition

PRIMARY TUMOR (T)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Tx:  Primary tumor cannot be assessed.
T0:  No evidence of primary tumor.
Tis: Carcinoma in situ — intramucosal carcinoma (invasion of lamina propria only).
T1:  Tumor invades the submucosa (through the muscularis mucosa but not into the muscularis propria).
T2:  Tumor invades the muscularis propria.
T3:  Tumor invades through the muscularis propria into pericolorectal tissues.
T4:  Tumor invades the visceral peritoneum or invades or adheres to adjacent organ or structure.
  T4a: Tumor invades through the visceral peritoneum (including gross perforation of the bowel through tumor, and continuous invasion of tumor through areas of inflammation to the surface of the visceral peritoneum).
  T4b: Tumor directly invades or adheres to adjacent organs or structures.

REGIONAL LYMPH NODES (N)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

N0:  No regional lymph node metastasis.
N1:  Metastasis in 1–3 regional lymph nodes; OR any number of tumor deposits present and all identifiable lymph nodes are negative.
  N1a: Metastasis in 1 regional lymph node
  N1b: Metastasis in 2–3 regional lymph nodes
  N1c: No regional lymph node metastasis, but tumor deposit(s) in the subserosa, mesentery, or non-peritonealized pericolic or perirectal/mesorectal tissues
N2:  Metastasis in ≥ 4 regional lymph nodes.
  N2a: Metastasis in 4–6 regional lymph nodes
  N2b: Metastasis in ≥ 7 regional lymph nodes

DISTANT METASTASIS (M):
M0: No distant metastasis.
M1a: Metastasis to one site or organ without peritoneal metastasis.
M1b: Metastasis to ≥ 2 sites or organs without peritoneal metastasis.
M1c: Metastasis to the peritoneal surface alone or with other site or organ metastases.

STAGE GROUPS (COLORECTAL CANCER)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Stage 0:   Tis N0 M0
Stage I:   T1 N0 M0; T2 N0 M0
Stage IIA: T3 N0 M0
Stage IIB: T4a N0 M0
Stage IIC: T4b N0 M0
Stage IIIA: T1-T2 N1/N1c M0; T1 N2a M0
Stage IIIB: T3-T4a N1/N1c M0; T2-T3 N2a M0; T1-T2 N2b M0
Stage IIIC: T4a N2a M0; T3-T4a N2b M0; T4b N1-N2 M0
Stage IVA: Any T, Any N, M1a
Stage IVB: Any T, Any N, M1b
Stage IVC: Any T, Any N, M1c

KEY BIOMARKERS FOR COLORECTAL CANCER
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

RAS/RAF Mutations (Predictive — anti-EGFR therapy):
  KRAS exons 2, 3, 4 (codons 12, 13, 59, 61, 117, 146): Mutant RAS predicts RESISTANCE to cetuximab/panitumumab.
  NRAS exons 2, 3, 4: Same as KRAS.
  BRAF V600E mutation: Predicts poor prognosis; encorafenib + cetuximab approved in second-line mCRC (BEACON trial).
  RAS/RAF wild-type: Eligible for anti-EGFR antibody therapy (cetuximab or panitumumab).

Mismatch Repair / Microsatellite Status:
  dMMR (Mismatch Repair Deficient) / MSI-H (Microsatellite Instability High):
    - Detected by IHC (loss of MLH1, PMS2, MSH2, or MSH6 expression) or PCR/NGS.
    - Predicts benefit from immune checkpoint inhibitors.
    - Pembrolizumab: FDA-approved first-line for dMMR/MSI-H mCRC.
    - Nivolumab + ipilimumab: FDA-approved for dMMR/MSI-H mCRC.
    - Lynch Syndrome: Germline MMR gene mutation — family counseling indicated.
  pMMR / MSS (Microsatellite Stable): Does NOT benefit from single-agent checkpoint inhibitor therapy.

HER2 Amplification: Present in ~5% of colorectal cancers, enriched in RAS/BRAF wild-type tumors.
  HER2-amplified mCRC: Trastuzumab + tucatinib (MOUNTAINEER trial); or trastuzumab + lapatinib.

NTRK Fusion: Entrectinib or larotrectinib (pan-tumor, tissue-agnostic).

PIK3CA mutation: Aspirin use may improve outcomes (investigational).
