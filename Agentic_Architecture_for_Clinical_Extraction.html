Scaling Clinical Data Extraction: An Agentic Architecture for Mitigating Hallucinations and Audit Risk

1. The Context & Baseline Architecture

The current transition in clinical data extraction relies heavily on combining foundational Large Language Models (LLMs) with deterministic models via a Bayesian Ensemble. This approach is highly robust and establishes an excellent baseline for initial Clinical Named Entity Recognition (NER). By bridging probabilistic reasoning with deterministic rules, it successfully solves the initial recall versus precision tradeoff inherent in processing unstructured electronic health records (EHR).

However, as extraction systems scale toward automated, claims-ready Hierarchical Condition Category (HCC) coding, the technical requirements shift. The goal is no longer just identifying medical concepts; it is ensuring absolute chronological accuracy, strict attribution, and Medicare compliance at scale.

2. The Scaling Challenges

Transitioning from "data extraction" to "automated Risk Adjustment coding" exposes several critical bottlenecks that standard LLM ensembles struggle to handle gracefully.

Challenge A: Temporal and Experiencer Hallucinations

Clinical notes are notoriously dense with negated historical rule-outs and complex family histories (e.g., "Patient's mother had breast cancer. Patient denies chest pain."). LLMs, even when functioning within an ensemble, frequently struggle with attribution context boundaries. Without strict upstream control, models will silently poison the downstream Real-World Evidence (RWE) dataset by confidently assigning active disease weights to negated or family conditions.

Challenge B: The "MEAT" Criteria Audit Risk

In Medicare Risk Adjustment Data Validation (RADV) audits, extracting a condition is legally insufficient for revenue capture. The provider must explicitly document that the condition was Monitored, Evaluated, Assessed, or Treated (MEAT) during the specific encounter. If an AI extracts "Diabetes" from a "Past Medical History" block but the provider took no action on it during the visit, Medicare considers that code invalid. Submitting it results in compliance violations and financial clawbacks.

Challenge C: Compute Routing and Financial Scale

Executing a full Bayesian ensemble (calling multiple APIs concurrently) takes several seconds and incurs high token costs per note. Applying this heavy compute layer universally—even to simple routine checkups—results in unnecessary financial bloat.

3. The Proposed Solution: An Intercepting Agentic Framework

To resolve these bottlenecks, we propose wrapping the core Bayesian Ensemble in an Agentic Framework. Rather than trying to force the LLM to perfect its extraction, we introduce deterministic, specialized "Sub-Agents" that intercept failure modes before they reach the claims engine.

3.1. The Context Pre-Processor Agent

Before the ensemble expends compute on disambiguation, an upstream agent runs strictly on the extracted tokens to classify the Experiencer (Patient vs. Family) and Status (Active vs. Historical). If an entity is flagged as negated, it is immediately dropped from the pipeline, completely neutralizing temporal hallucinations.

3.2. The MEAT Validation Sub-Agent

Once an active condition is mapped to an ICD-10 code, a secondary agent scans the localized context of the extraction. It specifically hunts for cryptographic proof of clinical action (dosage adjustments, lab orders, referrals). If proof is found, it attaches the exact verbatim string to the payload as an audit trail. If no proof is found, the agent explicitly zeroes out the financial Risk Adjustment Factor (RAF) weight.

Conclusion

By implementing a multi-agent framework that wraps the extraction ensemble, organizations can eliminate the noise generated by LLM hallucinations and automate Risk Adjustment coding with mathematical certainty. The inclusion of the MEAT validation sub-agent alone transforms a standard RWE pipeline into an audit-proof claims engine.
